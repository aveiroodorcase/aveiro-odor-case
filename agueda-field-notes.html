Field Notes · Outside Azurva

A Saturday walk in Águeda that we had to cut short

17 October 2026 · Águeda – Oronhe · Two people observing

My husband and I travelled to Águeda on a pleasant Saturday to walk beside the river and through the fields. We expected a peaceful afternoon outdoors. Instead, we kept encountering unpleasant smells — and eventually turned back earlier than we had planned.

Two kinds of smell

Near the start of the riverside path, close to a small technical structure, we noticed a strong sewage-like smell. Further along, near a watercourse, we saw water flowing from an opening near the bank towards the stream, with some whitish water or foam nearby. There was a particularly unpleasant sewage-like odour there. We do not know what the structure is or where the water came from.

Across more open stretches by the fields, we both noticed another, more widespread smell. To me it was like sewage or waste mixed with something burnt — remarkably similar in character to smells I have experienced in Azurva. We also passed a heap of manure, but its ordinary manure smell was clearly different from the smell that troubled us. That comparison does not establish the origin of either odour.

Why we turned back

As we continued along the open fields, the smell seemed to grow stronger and I began to feel increasingly unwell. We still had time to walk further, but I decided we should stop and return to the town, hoping the air would be better there. On the way back I began to feel better. In central Águeda we could still notice a weaker smell, mixed with other town smells.

Our walk was cut short, and what should have been an enjoyable day out was spoiled. Both of us noticed the odours; the physical discomfort described here is my personal experience.

Where we noticed it

These notes record selected locations and times from that afternoon. The location labels are descriptive, not identifications of emission sources. Times are approximate and local to Portugal.

  1. 15:15 — Start of walk (40.572337, -8.452455): unpleasant odour noticed.
  2. 16:27 — Water flow (40.574134, -8.460531): unpleasant odour noticed.
  3. 16:41 — Open fields (40.572895, -8.466322): unpleasant odour noticed.
  4. 17:03 — Near homes (40.573802, -8.464267): unpleasant odour noticed.
  5. 17:10 — Sheltered path (40.572900, -8.459998): no odour noticed.
  6. 17:14 — Open path (40.572215, -8.457241): unpleasant odour noticed.
  7. 17:22 — Near start (40.572305, -8.452436): unpleasant odour noticed.
  8. 18:23 — Town centre (40.575331, -8.447077): faint similar odour.
  9. 18:58 — Oronhe station (40.572529, -8.476824): no odour noticed.

The sheltered, tree-lined section around 17:10 had no noticeable smell. On a more open section a few minutes later, we noticed it again. We felt wind in the open fields, but did not measure its direction or speed there. Later, at Oronhe station, we noticed no smell despite the train windows being open.

What happens next

On 17 October I sent a report about the initial locations to AdRA, copied to Águeda City Council. I am keeping the observations from this visit separate from my longer-running Azurva odour log. Other correspondence will only be marked as sent after it has actually been submitted.

What this does not establish

This was a single afternoon of personal observations, not air or water sampling. A similar smell in Águeda and Azurva does not prove a shared source. The observed water flow has not been shown to be an illegal discharge, and no company or facility is identified as responsible.
