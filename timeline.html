What happened

How I started
asking for answers

I began by asking other residents. Then I started keeping records and writing to the authorities. These are the main moments, from our first weeks in Azurva to the replies I received. The letters and case numbers are kept separately in Official Actions and Documents.

July 2026

We moved to Azurva

My husband and I moved to Azurva in July. Soon afterwards, we began noticing unpleasant smells. Some episodes made it difficult to open our windows. I cannot give an exact date for the first time we noticed it.

28–30 July 2026

The smell kept coming back

During our first weeks here, we noticed the smell again and again. I later described these days in my complaint. They helped me realise that this was something I needed to keep track of.

Brief record →
31 July 2026

I asked other residents

On 31 July, I posted in the public Facebook group “Aveiro - A mais Bela das Regiões” after noticing a very strong, unpleasant chemical- or burnt-like smell on 29, 30 and 31 July. I asked whether people in Azurva, Eixo, Esgueira or Cacia had noticed it too, and whether anyone knew its possible origin. I said I intended to report the situation to the authorities; the post itself is not evidence that those reports had already been sent.

13 August 2026

I started keeping a journal

I noted a very strong smell in Azurva. This is the first entry in the journal I now keep. I began recording what I noticed so I could give the authorities something more useful than a general complaint.

16 August 2026

I contacted SEPNA/GNR

I sent my first formal complaint about the recurring smells in Azurva. I described what we had experienced and asked for the problem to be investigated. This was the start of my official correspondence.

Brief record →
5 September 2026

I opened an iFAMA complaint

I submitted a complaint through iFAMA on 5 September. It was registered and assigned to CCDR Centro. I wanted the recurring smells investigated. Registration confirmed that my complaint had entered the system, not that an inspection had happened.

Brief record →
5 September 2026

I asked the city council for help

I asked Aveiro City Council whether others had complained, whether any checks had taken place and what it could do. I referred to my existing complaints. I do not have a detailed municipal reply to share here.

Brief record →
13 September 2026

The smell reached the centre of Aveiro

Until then, I had mostly experienced this awful smell around Azurva. But that morning, I could smell it inside the bus as we passed through Esgueira. When I reached Aveiro’s railway station, it was still there. I was shocked to find the same stench in a place I had always thought of as free from it. Standing by the beautiful station that tourists love to photograph, I made my first Facebook video about the smell. How could such a lovely city smell so bad?

14 September 2026

I wrote to Navigator

When I first smelled that awful stench after moving here, I was shocked. I had no idea what it was, so I asked local residents on Facebook. Some said people had been talking about the so-called “Cacia smell” for years and suggested pulp production as a possible cause. I also smelled it very strongly from the train right beside Navigator’s pulp mill in Cacia. Later I found company reports about past odour problems and efforts to reduce them. That is why I wrote to Navigator to ask questions — not to accuse it.

17 September 2026

GNR replied

GNR replied to my complaint in September. I kept the letter with my private records. Receiving a reply was a step in the process, but I still did not have an explanation for the recurring smell.

20 September 2026

I asked for a coordinated investigation

As the smell continued, I sent IGAMAOT a summary of what had happened. I asked for the authorities to work together and check while the smell was present. I cannot confirm that an inspection followed this request.

Brief record →
25 September 2026

I heard from the public-health service

USP Aveiro acknowledged my earlier message about the recurring smells. I replied with more observations and my journal. I wanted the health questions taken seriously; I do not have a completed assessment to share here.

Brief record →
30 September 2026

CCDR Centro passed my complaint to APA

CCDR Centro’s letter named Navigator Pulp Aveiro in Cacia and said the matter had been sent to APA to look into possible emissions problems or an unusual event at the mill. CCDR Centro identified the facility in its own letter; it did not establish that Navigator caused my reported smells. I am still waiting for answers.

Brief record →
2 October 2026

I shared an update after CCDR Centro’s reply

In a post to a local Facebook group, I described my odour journal, the authorities I had contacted and CCDR Centro’s response. CCDR Centro had identified Navigator Pulp Aveiro, Cacia, in its analysis and forwarded the matter to APA for further examination. That referral did not establish the source of the smells. I also asked why the issue had received so little journalistic attention.

I asked APA for the records

I asked APA for environmental records relevant to my complaint, including monitoring and inspection records. I sent a signed request that evening. The letters available here do not show that the records were supplied.

6 October 2026

Navigator sent its response

I received a response from Navigator on 7 October, in a letter dated 6 October. I keep it privately. I still do not know exactly where the recurring smell comes from and am continuing to ask for answers.

8 October 2026

I asked for an urgent investigation

After especially strong smells on 8 October, I asked several authorities for an urgent investigation. I received acknowledgements afterwards. The replies available to me did not confirm that an on-site inspection took place during those episodes.

Brief record →
13 October 2026

I asked CCDR Centro to investigate again

After another very strong smell on 12 October, I wrote to CCDR Centro again. I pointed out that even without general legal limits for odours, rules exist to reduce certain industrial smells. I asked what had actually been done since my complaint was sent to APA.

CCDR Centro automatically confirmed receipt of my email that morning. Later, its Aveiro office proposed an on-site inspection near Azurva. I replied that I welcome the inspection, but asked them to work in public areas and communicate with me by email for privacy reasons. The visit has not yet happened, and I have no inspection findings.

13 October 2026

I submitted a complaint to the European Commission

I asked the Commission to review how Portugal applies EU environmental rules to the recurring odours. I attached an official CCDR Centro letter and my observation summary. The online portal confirmed submission. On 14 October, the Commission's environment department confirmed registration under reference CPLT(2026)03536. The complaint is awaiting assessment; this is not yet an investigation or a decision.

19 October 2026

APA replied with a plan for further checks

APA said its review of Navigator’s reported July and August emissions found compliance with the applicable limits in the periods checked. It also said this does not rule out occasional bad smells. September monitoring information was not yet available for its review.

APA said it would ask Navigator about possible equipment problems or incidents, contact CCDR Centro and local authorities about other reports, and consider other potential industrial sources. These were planned enquiries, not completed findings. The source of the smell remains unknown.

Brief record →
20 October 2026

I asked APA to consider the Cacia wastewater plant too

I replied to APA’s letter, asking it to consider the regional ETAR de Cacia, operated by Águas do Centro Litoral, as a possible source alongside Navigator. I also shared my public case website and raised a question about how residents can report odours and see what happens to complaints. I explained that investigation and collecting evidence from other residents must remain the responsibility of competent authorities, not an individual resident.

This was a request to widen the checks, not a finding that the treatment plant is responsible. APA replied on 21 October; see the next entry.

Brief record →
21 October 2026

APA replied about ETAR de Cacia and public complaint records

APA said it would forward the concerns about ETAR de Cacia to its water-region administration, ARH-Centro, for examination. This is a proposed referral, not confirmation that an inspection has happened or that the wastewater plant caused the smells.

APA advised that reports of possible environmental offences should be submitted through iFAMA. It confirmed there is no public platform where everyone can consult all registered complaints for a particular locality, and acknowledged the value of accessible reporting and greater public transparency. APA said it would communicate further developments, without giving a date.

The Portuguese President’s office forwarded my letter to the Prime Minister’s office

The letter, signed by the Head of the Civil Office, does not report an inspection, order an investigation or confirm any action by the Prime Minister’s office.

21 October 2026

Severe night-time odour entered my home

At around 22:51 in Azurva, I noticed an exceptionally strong putrid odour, rated 5+/5 on my subjective scale. At approximately 23:05 it remained noticeable indoors even with the windows closed. Its origin remains unidentified. I sent an urgent email to GNR/SEPNA, copying relevant public authorities; sending the report does not confirm an inspection.

Brief record →
22 October 2026

I updated my iFAMA case and sought independent support

I added new facts and three official letters to iFAMA case RD1835/2026. The portal displayed the new entry dated 22 October; its activity history still showed only the assignment to CCDR Centro on 5 September and seven earlier submissions of new facts by me. No inspection findings appeared in that visible history, which does not establish that none took place.

I also followed up with the Portuguese Ombudsman on my 22 September request, and contacted GEMAC at the University of Aveiro for scientific guidance and Aveiro On Line News for possible press coverage. These messages were sent; no substantive reply to these new contacts is recorded yet.

Brief record →
23 October 2026

Strong daytime odour; urgent report and replies from GNR/SEPNA

At approximately 10:20 I detected a very intense putrid smell in Azurva (subjectively 5+/5). It was especially strong on our glazed balcony and detectable indoors near closed window frames. I still noticed it around 10:31. I emailed GNR/SEPNA while the episode was ongoing and copied other authorities, seeking an on-site check.

GNR/SEPNA replied that the new information was added to case 13057/2026, which remains under investigation. In a further response, it stated that the case had been forwarded again to the SEPNA team in Aveiro and that I should await investigation results. Neither message confirmed whether officers attended during the episode. I asked directly about an on-site visit and shared the exchange with the relevant authorities.

I also sent an urgent update to Notícias de Aveiro, Diário de Aveiro and Aveiro On Line News (OLN). OLN had acknowledged my earlier message and said it would look into the matter, without committing to coverage.

23 October 2026

I asked other residents on Reddit about their experiences

I posted a public question in r/aveiro asking whether residents of Azurva, Eixo, Cacia and nearby areas experience similar smells, when they notice them, and whether they have complained. I also asked about interest in a joint request for independent investigation. A crosspost to r/portugal was flagged for moderator review, so wider publication there was not confirmed.

23 October 2026

Diário de Aveiro reports on recurring odours and publishes a statement from CCDR Centro

In an article by João Peixinho published on 23 October, Diário de Aveiro described recurrent chemical-like or burnt odours reported in Aveiro, Azurva and Cacia, identified the origin as unknown, and linked to this resident-led documentation website. Ribau Esteves, president of CCDR Centro, told the newspaper that he would “acionar mecanismos para tentar perceber o que se passa” — activate mechanisms to try to understand what is happening. This is a reported intention, not confirmation that any inspection has taken place or that a source has been identified.

24 October 2026

Public consultation submission confirmed for Navigator Tissue Aveiro expansion (AIA 3956)

I submitted comments through the official Participa portal concerning the proposed second tissue paper machine. An automated confirmation email dated 24 October confirms successful submission. My comments ask for assessment of existing odour reports, cumulative air-quality effects and appropriate monitoring and mitigation measures, without attributing the existing smells to any operator. The consultation report is pending.

26 October 2026

CCDR Centro proposes a site visit; written response provides public locations

An email from CCDR Centro's Aveiro service proposed an inspection of the surroundings of the resident's home on 28 October at 10:00 and requested confirmation of a meeting place. On 26 October, I replied in writing with a public meeting-free observation point — Urb. Azurva B bus stop on EN230 (map) — and another area where I have personally noticed an odour, near the Lidl at Aveiro Retail Park. I explained that the episodes are intermittent, often late at night, so a single morning visit without odour cannot rule out the reported problem. I asked what the inspection methodology and further steps will be, and stated that I prefer to provide any additional information in writing rather than attend in person. I also drew attention to the residents' public discussion.

Status: visit proposed, not confirmed here as completed. No inspection report, definitive source identification or remedial plan has yet been communicated to me; this does not establish that authorities have performed no other checks.
