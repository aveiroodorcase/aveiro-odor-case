Investigation priorities

Questions that need evidence.

The aim is a traceable investigation, practical mitigation where warranted, and a way to verify whether conditions improve.

01

Which sources explain the recurring odours?

Investigate plausible sources independently, including the possibility of more than one source. Similar descriptions and proximity do not establish common origin.

02

Can individual episodes be matched to operating and weather records?

Compare dated observations with contemporaneous station measurements, local conditions, operational events and relevant emission records. Distinguish observations from forecasts and reconstructed weather.

03

What do the official records actually establish?

Publish verified, redacted correspondence and inspection findings, with dates, scope, methodology and explicit limits. Acknowledgement of a complaint is not proof of its cause.

04

What monitoring is needed during an episode?

Define a documented investigation and sampling approach suited to intermittent events, with transparent measurement locations, methods and uncertainty.

05

What changes would reduce the nuisance?

Once a source and mechanism are established, identify specific measures, responsibility and a timetable. Avoid presuming which operator or process is responsible in advance.

06

How will improvement be verified?

Set transparent follow-up criteria, record both odour and no-odour observations, and publish monitoring results and the limits of any comparison.

07

Who will coordinate the response?

Clarify the responsible bodies, routes for sharing evidence and follow-up. The proposed CIRA submission remains pending confirmation.
