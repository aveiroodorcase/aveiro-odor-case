Speaking publicly about
the odour problem

Public Awareness — my appeals to other residents, the posts I shared and the record I continue to keep.

I began speaking publicly about the smell on 31 July 2026, before my first formal complaint. I wanted to find out whether other residents had noticed it too, and to keep the problem visible while I sought answers.

These three posts in the public group “Aveiro - A mais Bela das Regiões” trace that effort: an initial appeal, an awareness video and a detailed update on the case.

31 July 2026

My first public appeal about the odour

Post title in English: An unpleasant smell in the air — Azurva

After experiencing strong, unpleasant odours in Azurva on 29, 30 and 31 July, I decided to ask other residents whether they had noticed the same problem.

I published my first Facebook post in the local group “Aveiro - A mais Bela das Regiões”, describing a strong smell resembling chemicals and burning.

I asked whether people in Azurva, Eixo, Esgueira or Cacia had experienced similar episodes and expressed my intention to report the situation to the relevant authorities.

This was my first attempt to bring public attention to the problem, before my first formal complaint to SEPNA/GNR on 16 August 2026.

13 September 2026

A public awareness video about the odour problem

Post title in English: Aveiro looks beautiful. So why does the air smell so bad?

As the episodes continued, I published a video highlighting the contrast between the beauty of Aveiro and the unpleasant odours experienced in parts of the region.

In the video, I explained how the smell affected my well-being and asked other residents to share whether they had noticed similar odours, including the location and time.

The purpose was to make the problem visible beyond individual complaints to public authorities.

1 October 2026

A detailed public update on the ongoing case

Post title in English: Update on the odour episodes in Azurva / Aveiro

By October, the odour problem had remained unresolved for more than two months.

I published a detailed update describing how the episodes began in late July, how I had been recording them and which public authorities I had contacted.

I explained that the case had been referred by CCDR Centro to the Portuguese Environment Agency (APA) for further examination, following my complaint through iFAMA.

I also described my attempts to draw attention to the situation through the local press.

In particular, I had sent an open letter entitled “What air are we breathing in Aveiro?” to Diário de Aveiro on 5 September 2026. At the time of this Facebook update, it had not been published.

I wanted people to understand that the problem had not disappeared and that I was still seeking a clear explanation and effective action.

A continuing public record

Documenting the episodes in public

I have also been using the comments under an existing Facebook post to publish dated updates whenever I notice the odour.

These observations include the date, time, location, estimated intensity and, when verified, wind conditions.

I decided to document the episodes publicly so that the recurring nature of the problem would remain visible rather than being reduced to isolated complaints.

This public record complements my more detailed observation log, which is maintained separately.

On this website, personal observation times and locations are generalised. Weather reproduced from the journal is explicitly labelled where the underlying station reading has not been independently rechecked.
