Asking for help

Who I asked for help
And what happened next

Latest updates first. For entries covering several dates, the most recent date determines their position.

I kept writing to different organisations because the smell kept coming back. Here is who I contacted and what happened. So far, I still do not know what is causing it.

12 November 2026 · New update

My petition on recurring odours has been entered into Portugal’s parliamentary system

After I submitted a petition about the difficulty of investigating recurring odours, the Assembleia da República asked me for identification details and the petition documents. On 12 November, its petitions service confirmed that it had entered the petition into the parliamentary platform.

This is registration, not yet a decision on admissibility. The petition should become available to follow on the Parliament’s website after referral to the relevant committee. My petition concerns the protection of my own rights and living conditions as a resident. A separate question about whether I may initiate a petition solely in the general public interest is still awaiting clarification.

9 November 2026 · New update

Three inspections. No smell at the time. Case closed. But the odours haven't been explained.

Inspectors visited Azurva and other reported locations on 8, 9 and 16 October. They spoke to people there, but didn't notice the smell during those visits. The complaint was then closed without identifying its source.

The notice also mentioned that I hadn't accompanied the inspectors. But what would my presence have changed if there was no smell at that moment? For months, I've recorded when and where it happens, shared locations and answered questions in writing. The odours come and go — often without warning.

I’ve now asked for a review, the inspection records and a clear explanation. I’ve also informed the Ombudsman and asked for an examination of the procedure. This isn't about blaming inspectors. They did visit. It's about finding a way to investigate a problem that can't be scheduled.

A bigger question: what can ordinary people do?

Imagine someone in Aradas noticing a strong smell after nearby fields are sprayed. Or people in Barra experiencing occasional manure-like odours. Or someone driving past Cacia who feels unwell when a strong smell appears. These are examples based on personal accounts, not verified findings about any source.

They can report what happened. But must they then be there in person when inspectors arrive — even if the smell has already gone? I have not found a general legal rule requiring the person who reports an environmental problem to accompany an inspection. If a particular rule applies, I would like the authorities to explain it.

But what if you can't attend? You might be ill, elderly, disabled, at work or unable to speak Portuguese confidently. Should that make your report less important? And what if you can smell something, but an inspector cannot? People perceive odours differently; a disagreement is not a reliable way to identify their source.

We need investigations that go beyond a single visit: repeated observations, weather and episode records, and — where appropriate — sampling and recognised methods such as EN 16841 or EN 13725. These methods are not automatically mandatory in every case, and no single test can identify every smell.

Portugal has some legal ways to address odours, but no general ambient-air odour limits. So what practical protection is available when the problem keeps coming back?

What does someone in Portugal actually have to do to live without these recurring smells?

Status: Review and information requests sent on 9 November; responses pending. No source identified. Private correspondence and personal details are not published here.

7 November 2026

The Portuguese Ombudsman confirmed my complaint is under review

The Provedoria de Justiça confirmed the reference Q/6204/2026 (D) for my complaint concerning recurring odours. The office said the matter is being analysed and that further information will be provided when possible. It may make recommendations to the responsible authorities, although these are not binding.

Result: A case reference and an acknowledgement of ongoing analysis. No investigation findings, source identification or corrective measures have yet been communicated.

Source: Email from Provedoria de Justiça, 7 November 2026 (original retained privately).

4 November 2026

The Prime Minister’s office passed my complaint to the Environment Minister

The President’s office had already forwarded my 23 September message to the Prime Minister. In a new letter, the Prime Minister’s office confirmed that it had sent my complaint about the environmental problem in Aveiro to the Office of the Minister for the Environment and Energy.

Result: Receipt acknowledged and complaint forwarded. This is not a finding about odour sources, evidence of a field investigation, or confirmation of a remedy.

Reference: Ofício 7065/2026 (4 November); Entrada 6532/2026; Processo A.24-79/2026. Signed by Pedro Perestrelo Pinto. Original letter retained privately.

21 October 2026

APA agreed to refer the ETAR de Cacia question to ARH-Centro

Responding to my 20 October follow-up, APA said that ETAR de Cacia is not covered by the Integrated Pollution Prevention and Control regime handled by its industrial emissions division. APA stated that the concerns would be forwarded to its competent water-region department, ARH-Centro, for examination. This is a stated future referral, not evidence of a completed inspection or confirmation of any source.

APA also directed reports of possible environmental infringements to iFAMA. It explicitly confirmed that no public platform exists for consulting all registered complaints in a particular locality, while acknowledging the importance of accessible communication and greater transparency of environmental information.

Result: APA promised referral to ARH-Centro and further updates. No referral receipt, inspection findings or source identification has yet been documented.

19–20 October 2026

APA explained its next checks; I asked it to consider ETAR de Cacia

On 19 October, APA wrote that Navigator’s reported July and August emissions complied with the applicable limits for the monitoring periods reviewed. APA explicitly noted that this does not rule out occasional unpleasant smells. September monitoring data had not yet been available to APA.

APA said it would request information from Navigator about possible incidents and gas-treatment equipment problems, coordinate with CCDR Centro, seek other odour reports from Aveiro City Council and Eixo e Eirol parish authorities, and examine other potential industrial sources. These checks were described as next steps; no completed investigation findings were reported.

On 20 October, I replied and asked APA to consider the separate regional ETAR de Cacia, run by Águas do Centro Litoral, alongside Navigator. I included a link to this website, raised the difficulty of finding accessible public information about odour complaints, and explained that I cannot undertake technical investigation or collect other residents’ testimony on behalf of the authorities.

Result: A substantive reply and a proposed investigation plan. No source identified, no mitigation outcome confirmed, and a reply to my 20 October follow-up received on 21 October, with further checks still pending.

13 October 2026

I submitted a complaint to the European Commission

I asked the Commission to assess Portugal’s application of EU environmental rules in response to severe recurring odours. I provided the CCDR Centro letter and a summary of my observations. The online form confirmed successful submission.

Result: Registered by the Commission on 14 October as CPLT(2026)03536. Awaiting assessment; no finding or formal infringement procedure has been announced.

8–9 October 2026

I asked for an urgent on-site check

The smell was especially bad on 8 October, and I felt unwell. I wrote to GNR/SEPNA, APA, CCDR Centro, IGAMAOT and Aveiro City Council and asked them to investigate urgently. The next day, I wrote again, asking for real answers. I also said I was considering a complaint to the European Commission, which I submitted on 13 October.

GNR confirmed my case was registered, and CCDR Centro acknowledged the message. I have seen no evidence that anyone visited during the smell itself. Aveiro City Council has still not answered my complaint about these odours.

Result: GNR and CCDR Centro acknowledged the messages. No inspection during the episode has been confirmed, and the City Council has not answered.

14 September–7 October 2026

I contacted Navigator and received a reply

Navigator runs a pulp mill in Cacia. When the smell first shocked me, I asked local people on Facebook what on earth it was. Some mentioned the long-standing “Cacia smell” and suggested pulp production. I had also smelled a very strong odour from the train right beside Navigator’s mill. After finding company reports about past odours and work to reduce them, I wrote to ask whether there could be a connection. I did not know the source.

The company sent a detailed response. It discussed possible odours from its operations but did not establish the cause of the episodes I experienced. I keep the letter private.

Result: Navigator replied in detail, but the source of my reported smells was not established.

2 October 2026

I asked APA for environmental records

APA is Portugal’s national environmental agency.

I asked for records of inspections, emissions and monitoring that might help explain these odours. On 19 October, APA provided a substantive written reply, but not all of the records I requested. See the 19–20 October update below.

Result: APA replied on 19 October, but the requested records were not provided in full.

Brief record →

30 September 2026

CCDR Centro named Navigator in its letter to me

CCDR Centro is a regional public authority. APA is Portugal’s national environmental agency.

In its letter of 30 September, CCDR Centro mentioned Navigator’s pulp mill in Cacia and said it had sent my complaints to APA.

CCDR Centro itself said that Portugal has no specific legal limits for odours.

That shocked me. There are still rules for industrial pollution, so why does this smell keep returning? The letter did not explain where it comes from or say that anyone had checked during an episode.

On 13 October, I wrote again after another severe episode. I asked what CCDR Centro and APA had done so far and pointed to existing rules for reducing some industrial odours. CCDR Centro automatically confirmed that my email had arrived. Later that day, CCDR Centro proposed an on-site inspection in the Azurva area and asked me to agree on a date and meeting place. I replied that I welcome a genuine investigation but prefer written contact and checks in public areas, without meeting near my home. I am waiting for their response. The visit has not yet taken place.

Result: CCDR Centro has proposed an on-site inspection. Not yet carried out; no findings received.

21–25 September 2026

I contacted the public-health service

USP Aveiro is the local public-health service.

The service acknowledged my concerns, and I sent my observation log. I am still looking for answers about the possible effects on residents’ health.

Result: The public-health service acknowledged my report. No health assessment has been sent to me.

23 September – 21 October 2026

I wrote to the Portuguese President

I described the recurring intense odours and their effect on daily life in an email sent on 23 September. In a letter dated 21 October, the Civil Office of the President confirmed that the correspondence had been reviewed and that a copy had been sent to the Office of the Prime Minister.

Result: Forwarded to the Prime Minister’s office; no government investigation or follow-up action is confirmed by this letter.

22 September 2026

I wrote to the Portuguese Ombudsman

The Ombudsman looks into complaints about how public authorities act.

The online form failed, so I sent the complaint by email. I have a copy of the sent message, but no registration number.

Result: Email sent. I have not received a registration number or a reply.

5–20 September 2026

I asked IGAMAOT for help

IGAMAOT is a national inspection body that also deals with environmental complaints. iFAMA is the online complaint portal.

I reported the problem through iFAMA and added further observations. The complaint went to CCDR Centro. I also asked for a check during an actual episode of the smell.

Result: My complaint was registered and passed to CCDR Centro. No inspection result has been provided to me.

5 September 2026

I reported the problem to Aveiro City Council

This is Aveiro’s local government.

I asked whether other residents had complained and what the council planned to do. So far, Aveiro City Council has not answered those questions.

Result: No answer from Aveiro City Council to my odour complaints so far.

Since 16 August 2026

I kept reporting new episodes to SEPNA/GNR

SEPNA is the environmental police unit of the GNR.

I first complained in August. In September, GNR told me it had checked a waste-treatment facility and found it operating within its environmental permit. But it did not say which facility was checked or when, and the reply did not explain the recurring smells.

As the smell kept returning, I sent more reports, including during strong episodes in October. I received confirmations that my messages or case had been registered, but no further detailed inspection results.

Result: One detailed reply in September. Later reports were acknowledged, but I have received no new inspection findings.

21–22 October 2026

Urgent night-time report and further follow-up

During the night of 21 October, I reported an intense putrid odour in Azurva, detectable even indoors through closed windows, to GNR/SEPNA with copies to other competent authorities.

On 22 October I added the episode and APA responses of 19 and 21 October, along with the President’s Civil Office letter, to my existing iFAMA case RD1835/2026. The portal confirmed a new entry dated 22 October. In the visible activity history there were eight records: one assignment to CCDR Centro dated 5 September, and seven earlier submissions by the complainant. No inspection results were visible there; this does not prove that no inspections occurred.

I also requested a status update from the Portuguese Ombudsman, contacted University of Aveiro research group GEMAC for scientific guidance, and wrote to Aveiro On Line News. These are pending outreach steps, not confirmed investigations or media coverage.

Result: New facts submitted and outreach sent; actual inspection findings and identification of the odour source remain unconfirmed.

Brief record →

23 October 2026

Live odour report to GNR/SEPNA and request for on-site verification

I reported a very strong putrid odour in Azurva while it was occurring, including its presence near closed window frames and on a glazed balcony. GNR/SEPNA confirmed that the information was added to the still-open complaint 13057/2026. A subsequent response stated the matter was referred again to the local SEPNA team in Aveiro, pending its investigation.

Neither response confirmed whether officers visited during the odour episode. I asked directly whether a team attended and forwarded the exchange to the other authorities. The lack of confirmation is not evidence that a visit did not occur.

Result: Case registration and referral confirmed; an on-site inspection during the 23 October episode and its findings remain unconfirmed.

22–23 October 2026

Follow-up to local news outlets

I contacted OLN on 22 October. OLN acknowledged receipt and said it would review the matter. On 23 October I sent a further urgent report about the daytime episode and GNR/SEPNA correspondence to OLN, Diário de Aveiro and Notícias de Aveiro. Media coverage or editorial investigation has not been confirmed.

Result: One personal acknowledgement from OLN; coverage pending.

Plans I have not carried out

I have deferred the possible CIRA approach indefinitely.
