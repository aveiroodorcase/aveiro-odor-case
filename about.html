My story

Living here.
Looking for answers.

This is my own account of recurring unpleasant odours in Azurva and my effort to understand them.

It started with everyday life

We moved to Azurva in July 2026. Not long afterwards, we began noticing a recurring unpleasant smell.

Sometimes the smell was faint. At other times it was so strong that we could not open our windows to ventilate our home.

I began writing down what I noticed, contacting authorities and asking other residents whether they had experienced it too. I wanted to understand where the smell came from and what could be done.

Months later, I am still looking for clear answers.

Why I share this story

My records and correspondence became scattered across messages, letters and spreadsheets. I created this website to bring them together and show how the case has developed.

I want an investigation that identifies the sources, explains what we may be breathing, and leads to practical action. I also want to know whether any measures actually improve conditions.

Two versions of the journal

My private master log holds the original observations, precise times and locations, available weather data and working notes. I retain it privately for the competent authorities when appropriate. It is not part of this public repository or its downloads.

The public log is a separate set of 9 weekly regional summaries, based on 98 observations. It shows frequency and intensity without publishing a sequence of my movements. Missing dates do not mean there was no smell, and repeated checks do not necessarily represent separate episodes.

I describe what I could smell and how strong it felt. These observations do not measure pollution, diagnose illness or establish which operator caused an episode.

What the replies tell me

I distinguish between a message being received, a request being referred, a reported check and an actual investigation result. I keep the supporting correspondence in Official Actions and Documents so that anyone can see what each reply says and where questions remain.

Navigator’s response discusses operational events and weather but does not identify the origin of all the recurring odours. I do not claim that any named operator has been proved responsible for all of them.

I have deferred the possible CIRA approach indefinitely. I have not submitted a complaint to the European Commission.

My safety and privacy

For privacy and personal safety, this public log presents observations at a reduced level of geographical and temporal detail. The original records contain more precise timestamps and locations and are retained privately for submission to competent authorities when appropriate. Public entries may be aggregated to prevent the reconstruction of personal movements.

I do not publish our names, address, phone numbers, email addresses, signatures, home coordinates, bus stops, routes or return-home times. The public weekly log also excludes source row IDs and weather times linked to my individual observations.

Facebook screenshots, profile pictures, original post titles, exact post wording and direct links are withheld. I share short paraphrases of my public activity instead.

The working Excel and private correspondence remain outside the public site. Downloads contain only the same weekly summaries visible here. This limits what this edition reveals; it cannot erase earlier copies from Git history, caches or other people’s downloads.

The site has no tracking scripts, visitor forms, third-party fonts or interactive location maps. GitHub hosts the static files.

Keeping the account accurate

I translate the public summaries into English, check dates against the available documents and state when an account is mine rather than an independently reviewed original. I do not fill in missing inspection results or turn intentions into completed actions.
