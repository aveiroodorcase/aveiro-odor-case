Still looking for answers

Where does
the smell come from?

I have noticed more than one kind of awful smell around Aveiro. The familiar rotten-cabbage or sewage-like stench is not the same as the heavy smell I experienced near Lidl in Cacia.

Could there be more than one source?

In comments on my Facebook posts, local residents have mentioned wastewater-treatment facilities, companies in Cacia and waste-treatment sites. These are suggestions from residents, not proof that any particular site caused an episode.

Where people live and which way the wind blows may affect what they smell. I cannot tell whether these reports have one source or several. But people have been talking about unpleasant odours in this area for years, and I still have not seen an explanation or measures that have stopped the smells I experience.

I want the authorities to look at all plausible sources, find out what is happening and take effective steps to reduce the odours.

Could the wastewater treatment plant in Cacia be another source?

There is a large wastewater treatment plant near the factories in Cacia. It handles wastewater from several municipalities, including Aveiro and Águeda. The operator publishes a planned capacity of 48,700 cubic metres per day, but this figure does not tell us how much wastewater the plant was actually treating in October 2026.

Wastewater treatment can sometimes produce unpleasant smells. Cacia is several kilometres north of Azurva, so winds from the north could carry air from that direction towards our neighbourhood. But wind direction alone cannot tell us where a smell comes from. Similar smells noticed in Águeda also do not prove they travelled from Cacia. Local drains or other facilities could be involved.

This question goes back more than ten years. In February 2014, concerns from residents about unusual smells in Cacia were raised in the Portuguese Parliament. One question was whether the nearby SIMRIA wastewater treatment plant could be involved. This was a question, not proof that the plant was responsible.

The operator later publicly rejected claims about pollution from the water discharged by the plant. But clean enough wastewater and odour-free air are not the same thing: a plant may meet water-discharge rules and still produce smells. Portugal’s water-services regulator, ERSAR, also describes equipment for removing bad smells from wastewater plants.

For now, we do not know which source is causing the smells we experience in Azurva. The factory, wastewater facilities and other local sources all need to be considered. The aim is to understand what is happening, not to blame a particular facility without evidence.

Sources: operator description and capacity · 2014 parliamentary question · operator's response regarding discharges · ERSAR technical guide on wastewater treatment and odour removal

Industrial wastewater treatment at Navigator Pulp Aveiro (ETARI)

Navigator's Cacia pulp complex also has industrial wastewater treatment infrastructure, known as ETARI. A historical APA basin-planning report describes treatment of industrial process wastewater and other site streams, with a stated capacity of 2,600 cubic metres per hour. That figure comes from historical documentation and should not be mistaken for verified present-day operation or discharge volumes.

The July 2026 operating regulation for Águas do Centro Litoral also mentions infrastructure required to collect industrial wastewater from Navigator Pulp Aveiro. How the on-site ETARI and the wider wastewater network currently connect, and which areas might generate airborne odours, need to be established from up-to-date operator and permitting records.

Industrial pulp production can also generate odorous reduced sulphur compounds in other parts of the facility. Wastewater treatment and pulp-process air emissions should therefore be assessed separately and, where appropriate, together. Their proximity does not prove that either caused the odours recorded by residents.

Sources: APA historical report (ETARI) · AdCL 2026 operating regulation · Navigator environmental-licensing consultation

What would help distinguish these possibilities?

The most useful information would be the current operating and odour-control arrangements for ETAR de Cacia and Navigator's ETARI; any recent complaints or inspections; maps of pumping stations and wastewater collectors; and wind observations checked against the actual times and places of reported smells. A coordinated source assessment matters more than assuming that any one facility must be responsible.

Portuguese air-emissions law provides for technically appropriate practices to eliminate or minimise odorous compounds where applicable or required by the competent regional authority. The objective should be effective reduction of any verified nuisance, not simply issuing a fine.

Source: Decree-Law 39/2018, Article 9(2)
