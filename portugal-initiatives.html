Portugal: what has already been done about odours?

Documented initiatives, projects and questions still unanswered.

These examples show existing tools and initiatives. They do not prove all odour problems are solved, or that the absence of a universal limit means no legal obligations exist.

2017

Parliament adopted Resolution 190/2017 recommending urgent action concerning environmental problems and odours in Arrifana, Santa Maria da Feira. A parliamentary recommendation does not establish that the problems were solved.

View primary source / documentation →

2019–2021

The European D-NOSES project involved São João da Madeira and Portuguese partners, participatory mapping and discussion of regulation. Publication of project recommendations does not mean a national law was adopted.

View primary source / documentation →

2020 · Parliamentary Resolution 53/2020

On 30 July 2020, the Portuguese Parliament published Resolution 53/2020. In points 4 and 8 it recommended that the Government define measurable odour-monitoring parameters and legal concentration limits, identify critically affected areas, and include odour-assessment results in an annual report. This is a recommendation to Government, not itself an enforceable odour limit. We have not established which recommendations were implemented.

Read the official resolution →

2021

The D-NOSES strategic roadmap described actions and proposals to improve Portugal's regulatory framework. The subsequent status of discussed national guidance and actual implementation still require up-to-date documentary confirmation.

View primary source / documentation →

2024 · Clarification by APA

On 30 January 2024, APA stated that odours were not regulated or monitored through the national air-quality monitoring network or at EU level. This refers to that monitoring framework and does not negate activity-specific obligations. It raises a concrete question about follow-up to the 2020 parliamentary recommendations.

2026

In the Azurva/Aveiro case, reports and administrative replies continue, but as of 3 November the complainant had not been informed of a conclusive source identification or effective reduction plan. This does not mean no official checks took place.

What could Portugal adopt?

An accessible procedure for recurrent complaints, coordination across possible sources, technical assessment of episodes, criteria for corrective action and transparent reporting. This is a proposal for legal review, not a claim that such duties already apply universally.

Method: distinguish adopted decisions, projects, proposals and verified outcomes. Implementation and effectiveness still need case-by-case review.
