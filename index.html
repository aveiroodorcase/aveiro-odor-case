I moved to Azurva and began noticing a recurring odour

Clean air is a basic need. It should not be something residents have to fight for.

I moved to Azurva in July 2026. Soon after, I began experiencing recurring unpleasant odour episodes that affect my daily life, make it difficult to keep the windows open, and sometimes disturb my sleep and time outdoors.

This website documents what I have noticed, what I have reported, and what responses I have received, so the case is easier to understand and follow.

Environment and livelihoods · Figueira da Foz

When jobs depend on industry, can residents feel free to complain?

In a 23 February 2012 Diário de Notícias report about odours near the pulp and paper mills of Lavos and Leirosa, some residents described strong smells but also stressed the importance of industrial employment to families and local businesses. Some interviewees chose not to give their names. One restaurant manager said the jobs had to be preserved.

This raises a difficult question: do people sometimes hesitate to complain because they worry that environmental demands might threaten jobs and local livelihoods? The article illustrates this tension, but it does not establish that workers were threatened with dismissal, that employers retaliated or that everyone was afraid to speak.

Protecting employment and asking for effective odour controls should not be treated as mutually exclusive. In 2022, Figueira da Foz's municipality commissioned an odour study involving IDAD, questionnaires for residents, atmospheric-dispersion analysis and field inspections — a practical example of taking complaints seriously.

What living with the smell is like

My husband and I moved to Azurva in July 2026. It is a beautiful place. But soon after arriving, we started noticing a terrible smell that kept coming back.

Sometimes it is faint. Sometimes it is so strong that we cannot open our windows. I sometimes notice it almost every day, or more than once in a day. It affects how I use my home and spend time outside.

At its worst, the stench is overwhelming — like rotten cabbage, sewage and rotten eggs, sometimes with a burnt or chemical-like note. It can make me feel physically unwell. My husband smells it too and has witnessed many of the episodes.

Where does the smell come from, what are we breathing, and what will be done about it?

What I wish I had known before moving here

When we chose Azurva, we had no idea that recurring odours were something people in this area had been discussing for years. You can visit a place, fall in love with it, and still have no way of knowing what the air will be like on a different day, with a different wind.

Since August, I have written to authorities, kept records and followed up repeatedly. I have received reference numbers and some replies, but I still do not know what will actually change or when. Meanwhile, the smell keeps coming back. The hardest part is feeling that something so basic as being able to breathe fresh air at home has become a problem I cannot resolve on my own.

When asking for help becomes another burden

In Portugal, residents can report environmental problems, submit petitions and ask to speak at municipal meetings. But what if you do not speak Portuguese fluently, cannot address an assembly, are unwell, are young, or simply lack the time and money to keep pushing?

I have written letters, documented episodes and followed up for months. I have received reference numbers and some replies, but still no clear answer about when the odour will stop. Clean air should not depend on a person's ability to navigate bureaucracy or campaign for attention.

The right to petition also extends to foreign residents protecting their lawful rights and interests. Portuguese Petition Law, Article 4 · Aveiro Municipal Assembly participation rules

A question for Portugal’s government and environmental authorities

Give me one good reason why people should have to breathe this for years. Just one

And if there is no good reason, why is this still happening?

Some local residents have talked about these smells for years. How did something so awful become something people are expected to get used to? CCDR Centro itself told me that Portugal has no general legal limits specifically for odours. Who will take responsibility for changing that — and for using the environmental rules that already exist?

Why I made this website

After reading older posts and discussions by local residents, I realised people had been talking about unpleasant odours in this area long before we moved here. I do not know whether every report has the same cause.

Sometimes I wonder: if this were happening regularly in the centre of Aveiro, would the problem receive more attention?

I started keeping a record, asking other residents about their experiences and contacting the authorities. I received replies and case numbers, but the smell continued.

I made this website to bring the story together: what we have experienced, what I have done and what I have been told.

What happened next?

I began speaking publicly on 31 July and sent my first formal complaint in August. Since then, I have contacted several authorities and Navigator. After especially strong episodes in October, I asked for an urgent check while the smell was present.

I still do not know exactly where the smell comes from. Here you can follow what happened next and see the questions that remain.

Latest, 23 October: Another severe daytime odour episode was reported urgently to GNR/SEPNA. The service confirmed the information was added to the ongoing case and referred again to the local team, but did not confirm an on-site visit during the episode. I also opened a public discussion in r/aveiro. Residents and visitors have since shared recollections of odours over many years, observations from other neighbourhoods, and differing views on possible sources.
